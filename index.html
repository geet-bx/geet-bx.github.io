About

A science with room to grow.

I have a deep appreciation for the technical precision of intensive, focused interventions. There is nothing more rewarding than the success of mand training, where a learner acquires the functional communication necessary to exert agency over their environment.

Beyond my current clinical focus, I'm committed to the expansion of Applied Behaviour Analysis into different areas of life. This science isn't limited to the disability sector — it has immense potential to improve outcomes in health, sustainability, and environmental conservation. As I grow in the field, I'm actively looking to widen my own scope of practice to reflect the versatility of our discipline.

A note on my name: it uses a soft dental ‘t’ from Sanskrit. I love it when people give the correct pronunciation a go — but I'm a bigger believer in effective communication than perfection.

Toilet training, when earlier attempts haven't worked.

Much of my clinical work focuses on toilet training for learners with intellectual, developmental, or learning disabilities, often after previous attempts elsewhere haven't succeeded. I design the clinical program and make the key decisions remotely, while parents run the day-to-day training at home — supported by technology that keeps everyone connected without the cost of constant in-person sessions.

It's the same principle behind most of what I do: keep clinical oversight where it's needed, and hand the family a system simple enough for them to run themselves.
